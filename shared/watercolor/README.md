# Shared watercolor practice figure

The practice figure (`figure/` in this repository) draws every practice from this continuous CC0 MakeHuman surface. It is shaped by MakeHuman's own CC0 young-adult female macro targets, mostly the Asian one, made slim with MakeHuman's own weight, muscle, height and ideal-proportion targets (about eight heads tall, with slightly smaller hands and feet and a longer neck), and skinned with MakeHuman's own CC0 default rig weights, folded onto 46 bones. The hip and shoulder hand-overs are diffused across the surface, and the thighs stop at the hip crease. She wears a Tai Chi silk suit as cloth shells skinned by the same rig: a mandarin top fitted over the chest that falls to below the hips with side slits, a stand-up collar and a placket of frog-knot buttons, long sleeves, and trousers gathered at the ankle; her feet wear black cloth shoes. The body under the top and the trousers, which never shows, is left out. Her head adds MakeHuman's eyeballs under the face painting, sleek black hair combed close and a neat high bun, all following the head bone. Attribution and pinned original source metadata are beside the mesh.

The painted textures (`skin`, `top`, `trousers`, `hair`, `trim`, `face`.webp) and the practices' backdrops (`backdrops/`, 1280 px) are generated paintings in Inner Breeze's illustration style: the originals, their prompts and checks are in `source/` (`provenance.json`). Breeze's preparation script moves each swatch to the character's palette, makes it tile without a seam, strengthens the backdrops' colour, matches the face painting's skin to the skin swatch and fades it to skin outside the painted face. The paper, pigment and luminous-wash images are the generated bitmap textures of the reviewed Three.js prototype; their provenance is in Breeze design/astral-projection/three/assets/README.md.

Regenerate from the canonical Breeze checkout:

```sh
build-queue -- python3 scripts/prepare-practice-human.py --output ../../modules/practices/shared/watercolor
build-queue -- python3 scripts/prepare-practice-human.py --output ../../modules/practices/shared/watercolor --textures-only
```

`anatomy.bin` uses little endian PFIG0002. The 24-byte header contains magic (8 bytes), vertex/index/bone counts (u32 each), and rest height (f32). Each bone is 32 bytes: role/side/finger/segment (u8), parent (i32), rest origin and tip (three f32 each). Each vertex is 48 bytes: position (three f32), normal (three normalized i16), UV (two f32, in repeats of the region's tileable texture, wrapping the trunk and head about the vertical, each limb about its length and the hair towards the bun), detail (two f32: on the face the face painting's coordinates, on cloth how freely it hangs from the waist, 0 at the waist and 1 at the top's hem), four bone IDs (u8), four normalized u16 weights summing to 65535, region (u8) and how much of the face painting shows (u8, 0–255). Triangle indices are u16. Regions are 0 skin, 1 top, 2 trousers, 3 hair and shoes, and 4 trim (the placket). The native loader validates the whole stream before creating geometry.
